How Elo Works

Rating System Update - October 25, 2026

Q2Stats is updating its rating system today. The new model places greater emphasis on individual performance against opponents, while also rewarding each player’s share of team damage. Ratings and leaderboard positions may change as the match history is recalculated.

Q2Stats uses a modified Glicko-1 rating system. Team Games and 1v1 have separate ratings, both starting at 1500, with RD 100.

1. What has changed?

The previous system started with the team’s result, then adjusted each player’s rating change using individual performance. Much of that assessment came from the player’s share of team frags and damage.

The new model focuses more on individual performance against opponents, while also rewarding each player’s share of team damage. Rankings can change because the systems place different emphasis on team results, individual contribution and opponent strength.

2. Individual performance

Your Team performance score is:

K = kills, D = deaths, DG = damage dealt, and DT = damage received. These are performance points, not rating points.

Active fighting matters

You can take more damage and still achieve a higher performance score. Productive fighting and offensive output can outweigh the damage you receive.

Every 100 damage dealt adds 7 points, while 100 damage received subtracts 4. Dealing and receiving an additional 100 damage therefore adds 3 performance points, before changes to kills and deaths.

4. Comparisons against opponents

Your final performance is compared with each opponent’s performance. Teammates are not compared against each other. Each comparison produces a result:

Similar positive scores, within approximately 5%, count as a draw. Outside that threshold, a narrow performance advantage counts the same as a large one.

5. Opponent strength and expected performance

Glicko compares your actual results with what your rating predicts. R is your rating, Rj is the opponent’s rating, and RDj is the opponent’s uncertainty.

The constant 400 defines the rating scale. At equal ratings, the expected result is 0.5. The factor gj reduces the influence of opponents whose ratings are uncertain.

Outperforming a stronger opponent generally means more than outperforming a weaker one. The model uses each opponent’s individual rating, rather than the opposing team’s average.

The underlying Glicko change is capped at −150 to +150, and rating has a floor of 900. Q2Stats applies its Team match-result adjustment before storing the final rating.

For example, if you and four opponents all have 1500 rating and RD 100, winning three performance comparisons and losing one produces an underlying Glicko gain of approximately 42.2 points.

Still under development

The model recognises recorded performance, but cannot directly measure every contribution, including positioning, weapon control and communication.

We will continue evaluating its results and refining the model through testing and community feedback.
